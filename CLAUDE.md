# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

`srlocations` is a Python SDK that builds 3D locations as objects and compiles them to `sr_core.scene` documents, which the scene-render engine (Rust, GPU) draws. `README.md` is for people using the SDK, and every Python block in it runs as a test. `docs/tutorial.md` builds a location and extends the SDK step by step; its blocks run as one script in `tests/test_tutorial.py`, which also runs the tests it teaches, and its pictures in `docs/images/` are copied from that run's `renders/` by hand. `docs/location-book.md` is the Location Book: every published location, its versions, pictures and known limits, and the test count. Keep it true in the same commit as the change; `tests/test_location_book.py` fails when a location or version is missing from it or it names a file that does not exist. Never use claude.ai Artifacts for it or for any other page: they lose information, so pages are files in this repository.

## Architecture

The pipeline is `Location` -> `RenderMode` -> `Compiler` -> `sr_core` `Scene` -> package -> engine.

- **Graph** (`nodes.py`, `location.py`): a `Location` holds a tree of `Node`s, plus lights, marks, shots and `paintings`. `Node.walk()` flattens it into `Placed` leaves with world transforms. `location.solids()` and `location.lights()` are what compilers consume. A node's `role` says what it is in the set, and a leaf's `Placed.roles` holds its own and those above it.
- **Registry** (`registry.py`): named catalogs of materials, prefabs, rigs, archetypes, modes and checks. `Registry.default()` installs `stock/`. Names are refused twice unless `override=True`. Plugins and libraries fill it; `location.add("stock.sofa", ...)` resolves through it. Entry points load in the order their `requires` needs.
- **Compilers** (`compiler.py`): `SceneCompiler` is the real render, `WireframeCompiler` projects edges to 2D. `SceneCompiler.draw()` is the one place a solid becomes an `object3D`, and passes call it too. Each compiler has a `mode` name that paint and decals are matched against.
- **Looks** (`looks.py`): the engine has no custom surface shaders, but a group's 2D effects and blend modes apply to the 3D inside it. So `StyledCompiler` draws the whole set several times in one document, one `Pass` per group (`Lit`, `Flat`, `Cel`, `Ink`). `Stage` is what passes share. A `Look` also carries `textures` and `materials`, which change surfaces without touching the location. Those tables and a `Flat` palette take material names or their beginnings, `"role:wall"` or `"path:room/north/*"`, and a path beats a role beats a material (`lookup`). A key aimed at solids changes the material's id through `Stage.aimed`, or two solids of one material would share the first one's result. `Look.spec`, `from_spec`, `load` and `save` are its JSON form, and a blueprint can hold them under `"looks"`. `Cel(cut="auto")` renders a small probe of the shot while compiling (`measured_cut`), so a compile with it needs an engine. `Ink(rings=)` splits a curved solid through `Solid.bands()`, one key per band, and the ink shader does the rest. Shaders are in `srlocations/shaders/*.fs`.
- **Motion** (`motion.py`): the rule is that a frame of a moving shot is byte-identical to a still of `location.at(t)`, and `tests/test_motion.py` compares them. Keys live in `location.motion`, not on `Shot`, so published digests cannot shift. `Clip` turns a shot into one key per frame, linear, at exactly `frame / fps`: rounding those times to 6 places moved about 1,300 ink pixels. Solids, lights and the camera carry keys, so every pass follows without knowing. `Placed.track` holds a moving leaf's world per frame and `Placed.within()` derives its parts'. Paint and the wireframe are 2D, so each frame's marks are drawn by the still's own code in a group or shape that lives for that frame, and a plugin's `PaintItem` needs no change; `Frames` then lets a frame's leading nodes that repeat the frame before live on instead, and only leading ones, so later nodes stay on top. `Node.posed()` makes a node at a moment and `Node.keyed()` normalises key values; the mannequin overrides both, blending joint by joint. A node whose parts change (`motion.shape()`) is drawn as runs, one set of objects per shape, each living for its frames through `Placed.span`, and a node's lights may not change in number. An actor's keys are applied in `Location._item_at()`. A moment keeps its source as `origin`, and its `placed()` gives each moved leaf the world it began at as `Placed.start`, or marks it `reshaped` when its node's parts changed, as `Clip` does; `Ink.surfaces` keys by those, because paths are not unique and a key must not change as a solid moves. `smooth` is monotone cubic Hermite per number, per axis and per pose control. `instants()` bisects between keys until nothing moving goes `STRIDE` (3 cm), and after the first instant `clashes()` tests only pairs with something moving in them. It reads `self.nodes` with the moving ones taken from what `instants()` walked, not `at()`, so a `pov()` or `frame()` shot does not solve a figure a second time, and it returns at once with fewer than two bodies, and checks only time 0 when nothing moving collides at its start or any of its keys (`_collides`), since figures with `solid=False` spent nine tenths of a dance's compile there. Solving the figure is nearly all of its time. A `Check` with `over_time` is run once on the moving location and looks between keys itself; the others run at each key. Motion blur is the project's shutter, and ink and paint groups set `motionBlur="off"`, because blurred ink stripes. `location.plant()` keys a figure's `holds` as a tuple, which `blend` steps rather than interpolates, so each moment solves its own legs and nothing is carried from frame to frame. An ease may be `"cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2)"`, which `Eases.__missing__` builds, so `Key.ease` stays a string. `camera.py` holds what a camera does over time: `Follow` with `lag`, `dead` and `ahead`, worked out by `steady()` in fixed steps from time 0 along `location._path()` (keys only, no `at()`), and `Shake`, seeded waves applied last in `_shot_at`. A shake on a still location keys the shot so the location lasts. `location.onion()` is one still with see-through `Figure.ghosted()` copies at other times, appended after the origin's nodes, which `placed()` keeps as they are.
- **Sheets** (`sheets.py`): a tiled texture on a box goes to the engine as a small OBJ with world-space UVs, written by content to the cache (`portable.cache_folder("sheets")`), because the engine has no UV offset. `Solid.faces()` gives the corners, and `Stage.lay` takes this route when there are any. Round solids still use `spans()`. A model is turned on import, so writing z negated reverses winding, and the engine culls back faces: a wrong winding shows the far side of a wall.
- **Paint** (`paint.py`): `paint_over()` adds 2D layers after everything else, positioned through `Projection`, so it ignores depth unless its layer has `depth=True`, which `location.cast()` sets by default. Then `Holdout` gives each mark a track matte: the solids in the way in black over a white `Card` at the mark's distance. `PaintItem.cards()` says where those stand, so `frame` paint and `Picture`s have none and stay on top. A wireframe gets no `Holdout`, and glass holds nothing out. `Decal` is a real solid, hidden by the depth buffer.
- **Models** (`mesh.py`): `Mesh` is a `Solid` over a model file. glTF, GLB and OBJ are read here for bounds, material names and wireframe lines; the engine's other formats need a `size`. A solid reports its materials as `skins()`, one per slot, and a mesh keeps the file's materials unless `material` or `materials` replaces them. A file's `doubleSided` arrives in the skin's `own.extra`, and every material a pass or `Holdout` draws in a solid's place must keep it (`Stage.sided`, `Solid.two_sided`), or that face goes from that pass only. `Solid.declare()` adds its asset, and `portable.bundle()` follows the files a model reads.
- **Cast** (`cast.py`, `srstudio.py`, `_srstudio_worker.py`): `location.cast()` puts a `Performer` on a mark as paint, so a character stands the picture's lowest opaque edge on the mark, and furniture hides it through `Holdout`. `SrstudioCast` is a plugin that reaches srstudio through a subprocess in that project's interpreter (`SRSTUDIO_PYTHON`, Python 3.11 or later) because the two pin different sr-core versions. It never imports srstudio and writes nothing into that repository. Pictures are cached under `SRLOCATIONS_CACHE`. The worker runs with `-P` so `srlocations/srstudio.py` cannot shadow the real package. A mark can hold both a mannequin and a character: `looks=` on each gives solid blocking in the reviews and the paint in the finished looks, as `examples/casting.py` does.
- **Catalog** (`catalog.py`, `templates.py`): a shared folder of frozen props, parametric `PropTemplate`s, shaders, locations and looks, versioned and digest-checked. A frozen mesh copies its files into `models/<digest>/`. A published prop writes `seats` and `moves` only when it has them, so a prop without them keeps its digest, and a frozen surface keeps `hollow`. A published look may only use `stock.` passes, because a catalog holds data and not code. `catalog/` in this repo is a published example, and published versions are not edited. A digest covers an entry's payload and not its tags or description, which is one reason seals exist.
- **Exchange** (`exchange.py`, `browse.py`): `seal()` signs a manifest of every file with `ssh-keygen -Y`, and there is no cryptography here to maintain. `fetch()` stages, checks and swaps, and `allowed()` is the one list of paths a catalog can hold, used by inventory, fetch and server alike, so a new folder in a catalog must be added there. `CatalogServer` is read-only and binds to loopback, and tokens grant whole catalogs because a seal covers a whole catalog. Every defence has a test in `tests/test_exchange.py` that fails without it. `browse.py` writes a static page, escaping everything from the catalog.
- **Windows** (`stock/prefabs.py`): `Opening.view` hangs a `Backdrop` outside a `Wall`, one card to a picture. `Wall.outside` says which side of local z that is, since a `Room`'s south and west walls have it on -z. `Building(interiors=True)` replaces its block with a front cut around the windows, a body behind and a shallow `room()` per window.
- **Mannequin** (`stock/mannequin.py`): `stock.mannequin` is a `Prefab` for reviewing a set with a body in it, and `location.stand()` puts one on each mark. `kind` picks a body from `KINDS`, as multiples of the man in `JOINTS`, and `seed` varies it, or picks a kind too. `location.pov()` records a shot from a figure's eyes. It has Unity Humanoid's 55 bones and 95 muscles plus six root channels and a roll at each foot, 103 parameters written `"joint.control"`, with zero at the T-stance. Unity publishes no ranges, so the clinical ones come from the sources cited beside `_torso` (the widest normal among them) and the rest are working ranges, a guard against a broken pose and not a verdict on comfort. Limbs are `Surface`s so a frozen figure keeps its shape, and they are `hollow`, so `MarksClear` and `ShotsClear` see through them. A figure faces +z at yaw 0 and a stock chair faces -z, so sit it at the chair's yaw plus 180. A `Pose` is a `Mapping` of controls with `sits`, and mirrors, blends and has a JSON form. Poses and kinds are in `registry.poses` and `registry.kinds` too, and `registry.readers` lets a blueprint fill them without blueprint.py knowing the mannequin. `reach_to()` solves a hand or foot by geometry (`kinematics.py`): the bend from the law of cosines, a hinge folding the way most of its range lies, the elbow or knee toward `pole` (by default the way it bends now, or `POLES` when the limb is near straight), then the root's three angles from the rotation, the set nearest the old values. Where that breaks a range it tries swivels 15° apart nearest the pole first, and only then the old coordinate descent over `CHAINS`. A finger moves the wrist to the point less the finger's offset with the hand turned as it was, again for up to `FINGER_ROUNDS` where a range keeps the hand from turning back, and is searched for only where that cannot get there. `keep` turns the forearm or shin's twist and the hand or foot back to their old orientation, as far as ranges let. It takes world points because props publish no grip points yet. `holds` (`kinematics.Hold`) are solved in `frames()` after the floor drop, which comes from the pose alone, so a held foot never lifts the floor; the cache key then includes the transform. The hips then come down for a held foot out of reach (`_lowered`, at most `LOWEST` of the leg, scaled by what is not let go), a held foot the hips leave `BEHIND` rises onto its toes about them, in proportion, all of `TIPPED` at three times that (`_tipped`), pivoting on its ball toward the hips in step with the heel, with the toes turned back flat (the way to the hips is taken from the ankle, as the gate is, since from the toes it reversed at heel-off and the ankle popped), and `let_go` blends a joint from held to free, which is how `plant(release=)` eases off. `hold(flat=True)` sets a foot level at its standing height, facing the way it points, which `location.walk()` uses for every step, from the figure's `stepping`, `passing` and `stride`. A walk through several points rounds each corner with a parabola of `ROUNDING` pieces cut up to a stride either side, keys `at` and `yaw` at each piece, and faces the curve's tangent, since a sharp corner with a gradual turn planted the inside foot facing the wrong way and dragged it. `balanced` adds a hold for each foot on the floor where the pose put it and moves `hips.right` and `hips.forward` until `_centre()` is `STEADY` inside `_support()`, going toward the support's centroid, for up to `BALANCE_ROUNDS`; a figure already steady keeps its frames unchanged. The masses are `MASS` (Winter, Table 4.1, after Dempster), split over a segment's eggs by volume, and the support is the hull of the egg points within `FOOTED` of the floor. `JOINTS_VERSION` covers the names of the joints and their controls, which srstudio reads, and a test pins their digest: a rename or removal is a new major version. A figure letting go may dip into the floor, so `_cleared` leaves it alone. The `man-5th` to `woman-95th` kinds are fitted to `SURVEY` (ANSUR II, cited beside it), are `measured`, and a seed never varies or picks them. `teen`, `child` and `toddler` take stature and sitting height from `YOUNG` (NHANES medians, cited beside it) and nothing else, so a seed still varies them. They are not people, and no mannequin stands in for a named character's approved proportions.
- **Figures** (`figures.py`, `framing.py`, `review.py`): `Figure` is what `stand`, `settle`, `pov`, `look`, `reach`, `repose` and the checks need from a body, whatever its rig, and `Mannequin` is one. A character is not a Figure: it is paint, and it goes where the figure on its mark settled (`location.standing()`). A shot from `pov()` or `frame()` is recorded in `location.sights` or `location.framed` and taken again when a figure moves, and in each moment of a motion. Framings are functions in `registry.framings`. `location.blocking()` gives marks as plain data for a 2D rig, and `location.report()` gives measurements with no comfort verdicts, because those depend on the task. Both read a figure only through `Figure`: `stature()`, by default the top of its solids, and `shoulders()`, where `arm_reach()` is measured from, and headroom is measured above a joint named `head` or the whole figure.
- **Interchange** (`interchange.py`): `save_bvh()` and `save_glb()` write a mannequin's bones and motion, sampled from a location as a clip is (frame n at `start + n / fps`), in a model file's frame, SDK (x, y, -z), with the root carrying the figure's place. A BVH's offsets are differences of rest positions, so every channel at 0 is the T-stance, thumbs too, and its root offset is 0 so readers that add it agree with those that do not. A rigid GLB is bound where the figure stands at the first sample, so a `Mesh` of it matches the figure, and `FACET` keeps each limb's faces within 0.5 mm of its egg. `skinned=True` writes one body instead, the same eggs bound at the T-stance (a copy of the figure with no pose, adjust or holds), its nodes resting there and its pose always in an animation, even for one key: a vertex is its own bone's at weight 1 and a parent's or child's by `smoothstep` of how much nearer its own it is, over `BLEND` of the lower bone's radius, the eyes and jaw never, the four largest kept. The engine draws the rest unless `animationClip` is set, and the SDK never sets it. `load_bvh()` and `load_gltf()` both end in `_retargeted()` and give a `Take`: bones named through `ALIASES`, with a namespace such as `mixamorig:`, `mixamorig` or `mixamorig1:` looked through (`MIXAMO`), the file turned about y so its left and right fall on the figure's, scaled by bone length, and each bone swung from the way it points at the file's rest to ours (`_bound`). Each joint is then solved from its parent as solved, so what one joint cannot make the next takes up. A glTF's bones are its skin's joints, its rest is the nodes' own TRS, not the inverse bind matrices, a parent's scale is taken out of the turns (`_rigid`), and its keys are resampled at `fps` or their median gap. `load_keypoints()` reads per-frame 3D points, as a pose tracker gives them, a point at each joint's pivot, and builds each bone's turn from the points (a hinge from how its middle joint bends, the chest turned about its shoulder line until the spine puts the shoulders where they are, a forearm's twist from the palm) before `_retargeted()`; a bone with no points keeps its turn on its parent. In `_controls`, of the two angle sets for a joint, each held at the ranges, the one kept has the fewest degrees from the turn wanted plus the degrees it turns from the frame before: by degrees outside alone a knee folded for one frame, and by the nearest turn alone an arm held over the head leapt to its other end. A control with a whole turn of range wraps instead of holding. It uses the mannequin's private `_solved`, `_local`, `_offset`, `_bulk`, `_parts` and `_chosen`.
- **Fidelity** (`fidelity.py`): `fidelity(points, take)` measures a take against the points `load_keypoints()` read it from, in pure Python since the SDK has no numpy. Both sides are taken about the pelvis and in leg lengths, so another figure's size does not count. `lag` comes from cross-correlating speeds, a joint that does not move left out; `speed` and `acceleration` are 95th-percentile ratios; `beats` is Bailando's Beat Align Score both ways over kinematic beats (minima of mean limb speed); `ripple` is what the take adds between 4 and 6 Hz, through a band-pass run forward and back; `slide` is how fast a foot within `FOOTED` of the floor moves; `fallen` is the share of frames in runs of `FALLING` or longer with the centre of mass outside the parts within `BEARING` of the floor, not `balance()`, because the floor drop sets only the lower foot down; and `held` comes from `Take.clamped`. It uses the mannequin's private `_centre`, `_support` and `_margin`.
- **Collision** (`collision.py`): `Solid.hulls()` gives each solid's body as convex shapes, and the rules for when an overlap is a fault are all in that module's docstring: one object's parts never clash, a node with `moves` may touch but not enter anything, set pieces may share `DRESSED`, and a node with `yields` gives way to set pieces. `location.settle()` moves a body clear or refuses it, onto a `Seat` if it sits and back onto its backrest, and `Renderer.compile()` refuses a body still inside something, unless the renderer was made with `collisions=False`, which skips the search. `settle_all()` does it again for every body, from its mark. `solid=False` on a mannequin opts out. `location.contact` (`Contact`) holds the tolerances for one location: a body may sink `sink` into a leaf with the `soft` role or a material in `cushions`, compared without `@version`. `location.faults()` gives each limb at fault, a joint's ball counted with its bone, and `Clash.buried` marks a limb wholly inside a closed `Surface` (`Surface.encloses()`), which no depth can show. `location.gap()` measures how far a limb is from the set along a direction. A `Mesh` has one `Cloud` per connected piece of its model (`Model.pieces`, welded points, each piece cut to its furthest points along `AROUND`), one round them all past `PIECES`, and its box when the SDK cannot read the file or it has more than `MAX_TRIANGLES`. Self-collision is a warning (`figure-tangled`), never a refusal.
- **Portability** (`portable.py`): a compiled `Scene` names local absolute paths. `package()` copies each file a document reads into `assets/`, named by content, and rewrites a copy of the scene. `Renderer.still()` always goes through this, so every render tests it. `Renderer.package()` gives a folder and `Renderer.archive()` a `.vpkg.zip`.

Extension points are `Prefab`, `Figure`, `LightRig`, `Archetype`, `Check`, `Library`, `PropTemplate`, `Plugin`, `Compiler`, `RenderMode`, `Pass`, `PaintItem` and `Performer`. New behaviour should arrive through one of these rather than through a branch in a compiler.

## Coordinates

- SDK world: metres, x right, y up, z away.
- Engine: x right, y down, z away, 100 units to a metre. `engine_position` and `engine_angles` convert, and nothing else should.
- Yaw turns +z toward +x, pitch turns +z toward +y.
- A model is turned 180° about x on import (`Y_UP_METRES` in the engine), so model (x, y, z) lands at SDK (x, y, -z). It is a turn, not a mirror.
- `geometry.py`'s products are written out for speed but keep `sum()`, which compensates since Python 3.12: plain `+` changes last bits, and published digests with them. `tests/test_geometry.py` compares them with the plain formulas to the bit.

## Engine facts that shape the code

- A document's order is materials, composition, lights, effects, and sr-core sorts it. `<lights>`, `<materials>` and `<effects>` may not be empty, which is why they are created lazily.
- `object3D` takes no effects. Effects and blend go on the group around it.
- Lights are document-wide and one camera is shared, so a pass changes light through `Pass.relight`, for the whole document.
- Effect `<param>` values must be strings.
- Relative paths resolve against the document's folder.
- A layer's `scaleX`/`scaleY` are ratios with a default of 1, not percentages. `object3D` honours `scaleX/Y/Z`.
- `materialOverride` is `slot:name` pairs, split on whitespace and `:`, so a slot with either in its name cannot be addressed, and a pair naming no material only warns. `node=` draws the first node of that name and drops its own transform.
- Every face of a primitive maps a picture 0 to 1, and a box runs its UVs a different way along each axis (`prim.rs`, `cuboid`). There is no UV rotation, offset or world-space mapping on a primitive, and `uvScaleX`/`uvScaleY` are per material. A model's own UVs are honoured past 0 to 1, which is what `sheets.py` relies on. A round solid still gets `Solid.spans()`, one upright panel per face, each with its own material.
- Any node takes `matte` and `matteMode`, and the matte node is not drawn itself. A group of `object3D` works as a matte, with the shared camera and its own depth, which is what `Holdout` uses on both engines.
- A shader that fails to compile is passed through, with a note and exit 0. `Renderer.draw` therefore passes `--strict`. `encode --strict` also fails on warnings such as a solid off camera for a whole film, so `Renderer.clip` looks for "not rendered yet" in the output instead.
- `Solid.facets()` is for ink: thin surfaces in the solid's local frame. A facet that `is` the solid must not apply its transform again. A box's slabs stop `SEAM` short of the planes beside them: sharing a plane with a neighbour's face, they fought it for pixels and ink drew specks.
- The engine source is the scene-render repository, cloned here as `../scene-render`. Its `schema/scene-render-1.1.xsd` is the reference for attributes.

## Testing

Occlusion, registration and tiling only exist in the pixels, so those tests render small frames and sample them. `tests/test_looks.py` has the helpers (`stage`, `pixel`, `near`, `flat_set`). PIL is not installed; pixels come from ffmpeg as raw video. Reading `Scene` nodes: `deep()` for a recursive search, since `find_all` covers direct children only, and attributes are Python properties (`node.baseColor`), while `node.attributes()` is a method.

A render can pass every test and still look wrong. After changing a look, a pass or paint, run its example and view the PNGs.

## Scope

The mannequin has rigid segments, skinned only in the GLB `save_glb(skinned=True)` writes, and its inverse kinematics is one limb to one point, or held at one, with the hips lowered to keep a held foot in reach and, with `balanced`, shifted over the feet, never turned. The engine can skin a glTF and animate its joints (`animationClip`, `<joint>` under an `object3D`), and a skinned `save_glb()` plays in it, but a `Mesh` of one is drawn and measured at the T-stance. Collision is geometric and happens at placement: the engine's `rigidBody` and `physics` are unused, a settled figure does not change its pose to fit, and `repose()` stops where something is in the way. Grip points and seats on more props wait on socket names from srstudio's `feat/sdk-rules`, so do not invent them. Poses are read from BVH, glTF and tracked points, by the bone names in `known_as`; only the Mixamo names have been checked against a published file (three.js r160's `Xbot.glb`). P0 to P3 and motion are done, so the backlog is empty.
